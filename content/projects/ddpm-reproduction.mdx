## The paper

A generative model learns what a set of images has in common, so that it
can produce new images that look as if they came from the same set. Diffusion
models do this in two directions. The forward process takes a real image and
adds a little Gaussian noise to it, again and again, until nothing is left
but noise. The reverse process learns to undo that one small step at a time,
so that starting from pure noise it ends with a new image.

The idea came from a 2015 paper inspired by nonequilibrium physics, which
was hard to scale. Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Models (DDPM), from
2020, made it practical. Its network is trained to predict the noise that was
added at a given step, rather than the clean image. The authors showed that
this gives high quality images with an efficient training procedure, and
that training resembles denoising score matching while sampling resembles
Langevin dynamics, two known techniques. Those are the paper's results.

## What we reproduced

We were a team of two in an MS course.

- **CIFAR-10.** We first reproduced the paper's image generation on
  CIFAR-10, the small color image dataset the paper itself uses.
- **New datasets.** We then trained DDPM on two other datasets: MNIST, to
  generate handwritten digits, and the Amsterdam Library of Textures (ALOT),
  in its quarter resolution version of 384 by 256 pixels, to generate
  textures.
- **Settings.** The noise schedule ran for 1,000 steps. The U-Net widened
  from 32 to 256 channels as it went down in resolution and used attention
  with 4 heads. We trained for 40 epochs on each dataset at batch size 64.
- **Output.** For each dataset we produced grids of generated samples and
  videos of the generation process, from noise to image.

## What we found

After the CIFAR-10 reproduction, one U-Net design and one set of training
settings ran on both MNIST, whose digits have a single gray channel, and the
color textures of ALOT. Both trained for 40 epochs with the same noise
schedule and produced the sample grids and generation videos described above.
